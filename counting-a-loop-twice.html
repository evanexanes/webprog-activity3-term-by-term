Counting a loop twice: when the slide and the textbook disagree

DISCRET · Year 2, Term 1 · 2 min read

Based on: DISCRET Problem Sets 4.a and 4.b, graph theory, submitted 2026-10-06

Problem Sets 4.a and 4.b were two Microsoft Forms on graph theory: 30 questions on three pictured graphs, and 15 on Dijkstra's shortest path. Each question was worth one point. Counting questions took a number, walk questions were Yes or No, and neighbour, adjacent, source and sink questions were True or False. On 4.b, distance questions took a number and two questions removed a vertex or an edge and asked for the new distance.

Use the slide's definition

The slides fix some definitions that differ from the textbook. A trail has all edges distinct, so going back along an edge repeats it. A path has all vertices distinct. Eulerian means a walk that uses every edge exactly once, and the slides let that walk be open, so a graph with exactly two odd-degree vertices counts. The textbook meaning, a closed circuit, is stricter.

Two points the slides do not settle

  • The degree of a vertex with a loop. The slide says "number of edges incident", which read literally gives one. The usual convention counts a loop twice. I answered two.
  • A Form captioned undirected but drawn with arrows. I answered it as directed, because the directed answer was one of the listed options and the undirected answer was not.

Neither choice has been confirmed by the instructor. I wrote both down in my notes so I can correct them when feedback arrives.

The method

I read each picture into an explicit list of edges first. Then I recomputed every answer in Python from that list: walk checks, degrees, Hamiltonian cycles by brute force, and Dijkstra with a heap. For "identify the path" questions I costed every option, not only the winner. For "remove a vertex" questions I reran Dijkstra on the reduced list.

The limit of the method

Python cannot check how I read the picture. Before submitting, I compared each weight and arrow direction against the screen one more time.
