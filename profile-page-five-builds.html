Five builds to get a profile page that looked like mine

WEBPROG · Year 2, Term 2 · 2 min read

Based on: WEBPROG Activity 2, submitted 2026-11-06

WEBPROG Activity 2 asked for a profile page with three types of CSS, a skills list, a contact card and at least three pages. It was due on 2026-11-06 at 1:30 PM. I started on the 5th, and the page went through five different looks before I submitted it.

What changed each time

  • Build 1 was a dark developer-style page with a labelled box-model frame around my photo. I did not want a box on my picture, and nobody had asked whether the page should look like my portfolio.
  • Build 2 used cream and denim, my header bar and a code window. I said it looked like AI slop.
  • Build 3 was bold and loud, in yellow, black and white. The colours did not look like me at all.
  • Build 4 put my portfolio's colours on the bold layout, in two rounds. The second came after I read which colours the live site really uses where.
  • Build 5 used the whole portfolio design system.

Why build 4 still failed

Build 4 had the right colours and almost everything else wrong. It had thick black borders, hard offset shadows and a huge uppercase name. None of those exist on my portfolio. Colour is the easiest part of a design to copy and the least of what makes it recognisable.

What reading the real CSS showed

I opened the live site's styles.css instead of looking at screenshots. The system was quieter than I remembered: one-pixel hairline rules, corner radii of 12, 16 and 20 pixels, soft shadows, Outfit headings at weight 600, and the denim gradient on only the main button, the closing card and the footer.

I also cut a "How this page is built" section that explained the CSS to the grader. If a page uses a technique, it does not need to announce it.

Next time

Before the first build, open the reference site's stylesheet and write down its tokens. Ask early whether the page should look like my portfolio. The answer was yes the whole time.
